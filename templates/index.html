    How Canopy AI works (fully local)

    1. Segmentation. Every pixel is scored with spectral indices (ExG = 2G-R-B, HSV hue/saturation/value, RGB, VARI) plus an 11x11 local texture statistic. A small Random Forest classifier, trained locally on synthetic labeled scenes (no cloud, no external data), assigns trees, grass, buildings, roads/paved, water and bare soil from those features. A majority filter removes salt-and-pepper noise; connected-component analysis deletes regions smaller than 0.02% of the scene. Masks keep the original resolution and align exactly with input pixels.

    2. Plantable mask. Grass + bare-soil pixels minus a 1 m safety buffer around trees, buildings, roads and water. Fragments below 0.04% of the image are discarded as noise.

    3. Tree placement. Deterministic Bridson Poisson-disk sampling inside the eroded plantable mask enforces strict minimum spacing, so markers never overlap trees, buildings or roads.

    4. Metrics. Percentages come from actual pixel counts at the stated ground resolution. Heat score = clamp(18 + 0.90·impervious% + 0.40·bare% - 0.70·canopy% - 0.20·grass%, 0-100). Temperature = baseline − 0.06°C per canopy % point (literature range 0.04-0.10).
